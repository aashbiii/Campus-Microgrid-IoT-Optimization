Problem Explanation

Energy Waste In Campus Computer Labs

⚡ The Problem at RCSS Kalamassery
  • High Continuous Power Draw: Computing hubs like CC Lab and Carmel Lab draw 2.5 kW to 3.5 kW during scheduled class blocks.
  • Early Practical Session Completion: Coding practicals frequently conclude 30–45 minutes early; students depart leaving 35+ workstations fully powered.
  • Unannounced Cancellations & Lunch Dips: Unoccupied computing halls remain fully energized with zero occupancy, causing massive baseline leakage.
  • Substantial Institutional Cost: Preventable idle loads account for up to 35% of total computer lab electricity waste on campus.
🛑 Why Conventional Approaches Fail
  • Manual Human Inspection Fails: Facility technicians cannot physically walk across multi-story academic blocks to verify empty lab rooms in real time.
  • Zero Sub-Hourly Observability: Utility electricity bills arrive monthly, obscuring high-frequency load surges and unoccupied waste windows.
  • Lack of Closed-Loop Control: Existing commercial monitoring systems only display passive dashboards—none automatically trip circuit breakers.
  • The CM-EOS Innovation: Combines real-time IoT sensing, predictive ML, and autonomous MQTT-driven relay actuation to close the loop.
Solution Explanation

Autonomous Demand-Side Optimization Platform

1. Real-Time Sensing & Send
  • Hybrid Edge Nodes: Genuine ESP32 microcontrollers (Wokwi) alongside virtual Python daemons.
  • Physical Electrical Consistency: RMS Grid Voltage ~230V, Current I = P / V, Active Power P = V × I.
  • Uniform MQTT Data Contract: Isolated public broker namespace with 5-second telemetry streaming.
2. Predict & Decide
  • Machine Learning Forecasting: Gradient Boosting Regressor predicts demand 6 hours ahead with peak alerts.
  • 3-Factor Idle Rule Engine: Power < 150W for ≥ 30s during scheduled timetable windows flags waste.
  • IPMVP Option C Accounting: Quantifies verified kWh energy conserved and avoided CO₂ emissions.
3. Act & Show (Closed Loop)
  • Remote Actuation: Automatically dispatches SHUTDOWN over MQTT to open the physical relay.
  • Verification Feedback: Confirms relay_state == OFF in subsequent telemetry packets.
  • Real-Time Dashboard: Streamlit UI with live Plotly traces and one-click manual restoration.
System Architecture

Multi-Layer System Architecture

🏗️ Edge & Messaging Architecture
  • Layer 1: Edge Computing Nodes
    • Wokwi ESP32 Nodes: CC Lab & Carmel Lab running embedded C++.
    • Python Virtual Daemons: Assumption Lab & Chavara Lab.
  • Layer 2: Communication Broker (MQTT)
    • Public HiveMQ Broker (broker.hivemq.com:1883).
    • Namespace: campusmg_x7k2/<lab_id>/telemetry and /cmd.
  • Layer 3: Telemetry Ingestion Service (subscriber.py)
    • Schema validation, sanitization, and concurrent write queue.
🧠 Analytics, Decision & UI Layers
  • Layer 4: High-Performance Persistence (SQLite WAL)
    • Write-Ahead Logging (WAL) decouples writers from readers, preventing database is locked crashes.
  • Layer 5: Machine Learning Engine (forecast.py)
    • Scikit-Learn GradientBoostingRegressor with lag and rolling features.
  • Layer 6: Optimization & Actuation (optimizer.py)
    • Autonomous 3-factor idle detector and M&V energy calculation.
  • Layer 7: Central Dashboard (dashboard.py)
    • Streamlit reactive application with 5-second live auto-refresh.

Hardware-in-the-Loop

ESP32 Firmware & Embedded Pin Mapping

🔌 Physical Hardware Connections
  • ESP32 DevKit V1: Dual-core 240MHz SoC with integrated 802.11 b/g/n Wi-Fi.
  • Potentiometer (GPIO 34 / ADC1_CH6): Analog wiper (0–4095) linearly mapped to workstation current (0.2 A to 15.0 A).
  • Relay Module (GPIO 26): Digital contactor control. HIGH = Energized; LOW = Open circuit.
  • Status LED (GPIO 2): Visual feedback of energized circuit breaker.
  • Real Sensor Path: In a live deployment, the potentiometer maps directly to an INA219 I2C sensor or SCT-013 CT clamp with zero firmware modifications.
  • Live Simulation Links:
    • CC Lab Simulation ↗
    • Carmel Lab Simulation ↗
💻 Firmware Execution Logic (sketch.ino)
  • Non-Blocking Timing: Uses millis() (5000 ms cadence) preventing MQTT thread starvation.
  • ArduinoJson Serialization: Packages active power, voltage, current, and relay state into a compact JSON payload.
  • PubSubClient Callback: Subscribes to campusmg_x7k2/cc_lab/cmd for remote control.
  • Instant Standby Clamping: On receiving "SHUTDOWN", immediately de-energizes GPIO 26 and GPIO 2, clamping active power to ~200W.

Predictive Analytics

Machine Learning Load Forecasting Pipeline

⚙️ Feature Engineering & Training
  • Historical Seed: 30 days of 5-minute readings per lab (34,564 rows in SQLite WAL).
  • Hourly Resampling: Aggregated to hourly mean power to capture macro demand trends.
  • Calendar Features: hour (0–23), dayofweek (0–6), is_weekend (0 or 1).
  • Autoregressive Lags: lag_1h (momentum), lag_2h, and lag_24h (daily diurnal seasonality).
  • Rolling Window Statistics: rolling_mean_3h and rolling_mean_24h moving trend filters.
  • Strict Temporal Split: Chronological 80/20 split. Strictly no shuffling to prevent lookahead leakage.

Predictive Analytics

Recursive 6-Hour Forecasting & Peak Alerts

🔄 Multi-Step Recursive Horizon Algorithm
  • Step t+1: Predict $\hat{y}_{t+1}$ using current lag and rolling features.
  • Recursive Feedback: Append $\hat{y}_{t+1}$ to feature vector: lag_1h = $\hat{y}_{t+1}$, lag_2h = $y_t$.
  • Rolling Buffer Update: Append $\hat{y}_{t+1}$ to rolling 3-hour and 24-hour moving averages.
  • Horizon Steps t+2 to t+6: Iterate forward to construct full 6-hour forward demand curve.
  • Campus Aggregation: Sum individual facility models to forecast total campus microgrid load.
⚡ Automated Peak Demand Warning
  • Early Warning System: Identifies max projected power: $\max_{h \in [1..6]} \hat{P}_{\text{campus}}(t+h)$.
  • Grid Dispatch Alert: Flags the specific peak hour (e.g., 14:00 at ~10.4 kW) to notify campus electrical engineers in advance.
  • Avoid Peak Tariffs: Enables pre-emptive demand shifting before penalty tariff bands trigger.
Decision Engine

Autonomous 3-Factor Idle Detection & Actuation

⚙️ The 3-Factor Idle Rule Engine
  • Condition 1 (Power Deficit): Measured active power $P < 150.0\text{ W}$ (IDLE_POWER_W).
  • Condition 2 (Persistence Duration): Deficit lasts continuously for $\ge 30\text{ s}$ (Demo mode) or $\ge 900\text{ s}$ (Production).
  • Condition 3 (Timetable Window): Timestamp falls inside scheduled active class hours (prevents false alerts at midnight).
  • Automated Action: Publishes "SHUTDOWN" payload to campusmg_x7k2/<lab>/cmd.
📐 IPMVP Option C Verified Savings Formula
kWh Saved = [ (P_expected(h) - P_standby) × (Δt / 3600) ] / 1000
  • Expected Baseline Load: Dynamically queried from historical average power for that specific lab and hour.
  • Elapsed Duration: $\Delta t = t_{\text{restored}} - t_{\text{flagged}}$ measured in seconds.
  • Carbon Accounting: Avoided emissions calculated at standard grid intensity factor: $0.82\text{ kg CO}_2/\text{kWh}$.

Technical Defense

Faculty Viva Voce Defense & Technical Q&A

Q1: Why GBR over LSTM / Deep Learning?

Answer: On tabular time-series with engineered autoregressive lag features, gradient-boosted decision trees consistently outperform recurrent neural networks. GBR handles non-linear step drops (e.g. 13:00 lunch dip) with zero lag, avoids vanishing gradients, requires sub-millisecond CPU inference, and trains in seconds without GPU hardware.

Q2: Why is SQLite WAL Mode Essential?

Answer: In standard rollback journal mode, writing locks the entire SQLite database file. Since subscriber.py writes telemetry every 5 seconds, concurrent analytical reads from Streamlit would trigger database is locked crashes. WAL mode decouples readers from writers.

Q3: How is Energy Saved Accurately Calculated?

Answer: We follow International Performance Measurement and Verification Protocol (IPMVP) Option C: Expected energy is estimated using historical hourly averages for that specific lab and hour, minus measured standby power during the shutdown window: $$\text{Savings} = (P_{\text{historical\_avg}} - P_{\text{standby}}) \times \Delta t_{\text{shutdown}}$$

Q4: Safety Interlocks for Critical Infrastructure?

Answer: Critical institutional loads (campus servers, Wi-Fi access points, emergency lighting) are physically isolated on non-switchable circuit breakers; only general student workstations are connected to the contactor.
